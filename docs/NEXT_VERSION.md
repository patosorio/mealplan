## 1. Full Nutritional Intelligence

**The vision:** Nouri should be the most nutritionally transparent meal planner
available. Not just calories and protein — a complete picture of what the user
is putting in their body, per recipe and per week.

**Per-recipe nutritional profile:**
Every recipe should show a comprehensive breakdown including:

- Macronutrients: calories, protein, carbohydrates, fats (saturated, unsaturated, trans)
- Fibre (soluble and insoluble)
- Micronutrients: sodium, potassium, phosphorus, calcium, magnesium, iron, zinc
- Vitamins: A, B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, B9 (folate), B12, C, D, E, K
- Phytonutrients: flavonoids, carotenoids, polyphenols, glucosinolates where relevant
- Amino acid profile: complete vs incomplete protein indicator, key amino acids
- Omega-3 / Omega-6 ratio for plant-based sources

**Why this matters for plant-based users specifically:**
Plant-based diets have known risk areas — B12, iron (non-haem), calcium, zinc,
iodine, complete protein. Nouri should proactively surface these. A raw vegan
user who is chronically low in B12 and doesn't know it is a liability, not a
success story. The app should be an intelligent nutritional guardian, not just
a meal generator.

**Weekly nutritional summary:**
Aggregate view across the full plan — are you hitting RDA targets? Which
nutrients are consistently under? Colour-coded traffic light: green (on track),
amber (slightly under), red (deficient). This is the kind of feature that
drives retention — users come back to see their weekly score.

**Implementation note (for when ready):**
Nutritional data can be generated by Claude with approximate values during
Phase 2 enrichment (fits naturally into the Planner-Executor pattern). For
higher accuracy, a future version could integrate with a nutritional database
API (USDA FoodData Central is free, Edamam is a good paid option). Start with
Claude approximations — flag them as estimates in the UI — then upgrade to a
verified source when accuracy becomes a product requirement.

## 2. Health Platform Integrations

**The vision:** Nouri's nutritional data should flow into the user's wider
health ecosystem. Food is one input. The output is how the user feels, performs,
and ages.

**Priority integrations:**

- **Apple Health / Google Fit** — nutrition logs from accepted meal plans sync
automatically. Users see their macro intake in their health app without
manual logging.
- **MyFitnessPal** — export individual meals or full plans to the MFP food diary
- **Whoop / Garmin / Oura** — future: read recovery and activity data to adjust
calorie and macro targets dynamically. A user with a hard training day gets a
higher protein target that day.
- **Cronometer** — niche but beloved by plant-based users who track
micronutrients seriously. Natural audience overlap.

**The data play:**
This is also a strategic data asset. Aggregate anonymised nutrition and
behaviour data (meal acceptance rates, regeneration patterns, shopping list
completion) is valuable to food brands, health researchers, and insurance
companies. Requires GDPR-compliant consent architecture before pursuing.

## 3. Agentic Features — The Meal Coach (v3)

**The vision:** A conversational interface where the user doesn't fill out a
form — they just talk to Nouri. "I have a half marathon on Saturday, I want
to carb load Thursday and Friday and keep protein high all week." Nouri
plans accordingly.

**Three specific agentic features:**

**Meal Coach:**
The user chats. Claude decides whether to regenerate a day, check the pantry,
suggest a swap, update preferences, or just answer a nutrition question — all
autonomously in one conversation turn. This is where LangGraph becomes
warranted (genuinely dynamic execution graph, not a fixed pipeline).

**Autonomous Shopping List Optimizer:**
Rather than a simple plan-vs-pantry diff, Claude reasons about ingredient
substitutions. "You have half a butternut squash and some black beans — here's
how to use both this week without waste." Ingredient coherence becomes an
active optimisation, not just a prompt instruction.

**Nutritional Gap Detector:**
After a week's plan is approved, Claude proactively identifies gaps. "Your
plan this week is low in omega-3 and B12. I've suggested two swaps — want me
to apply them?" The user doesn't have to ask. The system notices.

**Technical note:** All three require LangGraph's graph execution model —
Claude autonomously deciding which tool to call next. This is v3, not v2.
Do not architect toward this prematurely.

## 4. Monetisation & Growth

**Stripe subscription:**
Freemium model — free users get 2 plan generations per month, paid users get
unlimited. Stripe integration is the prerequisite for investor conversations.
D7 and D30 retention metrics with paying users are the proof points that matter.

**Per-user generation rate limiting:**
Even before Stripe, add a daily generation limit (e.g. 5 per day per user)
stored in Redis. Prevents API cost abuse from power users and creates a
natural prompt toward upgrading.

**Beta user acquisition:**
YouTube and Instagram short-form content showing the generation flow,
personalisation loop, and nutritional detail is the right acquisition channel
for this product. The visual of a beautiful meal plan being built in real time
is inherently shareable. Record the video after the two-phase generation is
live — that's when the 4-second reveal animation makes the product look fast
and polished.

**The data asset story:**
Every user interaction — what they accept, what they regenerate, what they
search for, what they buy — is a nutritional behaviour signal. At scale this
is genuinely valuable. Plant-based food brands, supplement companies, health
insurers, and nutrition researchers would pay for aggregate anonymised insight.
This requires a clear consent model and privacy policy before pursuing.

## 5. Plan Export & Sharing

**Print / PDF (already partially built):**
A clean print stylesheet exists. A proper PDF export with Nouri branding,
the full week grid, nutritional summary, and shopping list is the natural
next step. Users want to put this on their fridge or share it with a
nutritionist.

**Read-only plan sharing:**
A public URL (no login required) that shows a generated plan. The recipient
can see the meals and nutritional breakdown but cannot edit. This is a
viral acquisition mechanism — "my nutritionist sent me this plan" brings
new users in.

**Nutritionist / coach mode:**
A professional account type where a practitioner generates plans for clients.
The client receives a read-only link. The practitioner can see acceptance and
feedback signals across all their clients. This is a B2B revenue angle.

## 6. Mobile Application

**Platform:** Expo / React Native, sharing business logic with the Next.js
frontend where possible. Assessed and understood — flagged as premature until
web product is stable and has paying users.

**Why mobile matters for this product specifically:**
Meal planning is a mobile behaviour. Users check their plan in the kitchen,
in the supermarket, at a restaurant. The shopping list and pantry features
especially need to be native-feeling on mobile. The current responsive web
is a stopgap.

**PWA first:** Before building native, ship a proper PWA with a manifest,
offline support for viewing saved plans, and add-to-home-screen. This is
a few hours of work and covers 80% of the mobile use case.

## 7. Advanced Personalisation

**Seasonal and local menus:**
Beyond the location awareness planned in Section 23C, offer quarterly
"seasonal menu" suggestions — a curated set of recipes that make the most
of what's abundant and cheap in the user's region right now. Spring greens
in the UK. Mangoes in Thailand. Squash in the US Northeast in October.

**Fasting and rest day slots:**
A `type="fast"` option on `DayPlan` — an intermittent fasting day with
adjusted calorie targets and meal timing. Growing user segment, natural fit
for a health-forward product.

**Prep batching across days:**
"You're making chickpeas on Monday — I've used the leftovers in Wednesday's
lunch." Explicit cross-day ingredient reuse that reduces cooking time and
waste. The two-phase architecture makes this natural to implement (Phase 1
Sonnet plans the batch strategy, Phase 2 Haiku writes the individual meals).

**Nutritional goal tracking across weeks:**
A dashboard view showing nutritional trends over time — is your protein
intake improving? Is your iron consistently low? Requires the full
nutritional profile (Feature 1) to be meaningful.

## 8. Evaluation & Quality Assurance

**AI-as-judge pipeline:**
After each plan generation, a secondary Haiku call scores the plan 1-5
on "profile faithfulness" — did it actually use favourite ingredients,
avoid disliked meals, match prep time preference, rotate proteins? Store
as `meal_plans.quality_score FLOAT`. Alert when prompt changes degrade
the score.

**Gold dataset from user signals:**
Plans where the user approved 7/7 meals are high-quality examples. Plans
where they swapped 4/7 are low-quality. Extract these programmatically
from `user_signals` and `generated_meals`. Use as a benchmark when
changing prompt templates — run the benchmark before deploying any prompt
change.

**Nutritional accuracy audit:**
Periodically cross-reference Claude's nutritional estimates against USDA
FoodData Central for a sample of popular recipes. Track the error rate.
When error rate exceeds a threshold, switch to a verified data source.

## 9. Compliance & Legal

**GDPR / Privacy:**
User data includes dietary preferences and health-adjacent information —
these are sensitive in several jurisdictions. Required before EU launch:

- Privacy policy page
- Cookie consent banner
- `DELETE /users/me` endpoint that hard-deletes all user data
- Data retention policy (how long are signals kept?)
- Right to data export (user can download everything Nouri knows about them)

**Nutritional disclaimer:**
All nutritional values generated by AI are estimates and should not be used
as a substitute for professional medical or dietary advice. This must be
visible in the UI wherever nutritional data is displayed — not buried in
terms and conditions.

**Health app integration compliance:**
Apple HealthKit and Google Fit have their own developer programme terms.
Review before building integrations. Some data types require additional
app review approval.

## 10. Infrastructure Maturity

**Observability with trace IDs:**
Every meal plan generation request should carry a `trace_id` that links all
downstream operations — Phase 1 call, all 7 Phase 2 calls, DB writes, signal
logging, profile rebuild. When something fails, the trace shows exactly which
step and why. Essential as generation becomes more complex.

**Redis caching for taste profiles:**
Profiles rebuild nightly. Cache with 1hr TTL in Redis, invalidate on
`saved_meal` signal. Eliminates one DB round-trip per generation call at scale.

**Cost budget alerts:**
Set hard budget alerts in both the Anthropic console and GCP billing. One
prompt bug that causes infinite retries can generate a large unexpected bill.
Anthropic supports spend limits per API key.

**Pantry photo scanning:**
Gemini Vision can read a photo of the user's fridge or pantry shelf and
extract ingredient names and approximate quantities. High-delight, low-friction
way to populate the pantry. Deferred because it requires careful UX design
to handle errors gracefully (blurry photos, obscured labels, multiple languages).

## 11. Location Awareness & Seasonal Ingredient Context

**Priority: Medium. Implement alongside or after food profile questionnaire.**

**Problem:** Claude suggests ingredients without considering local
availability, cost, or seasonality. A coconut-a-day plan makes sense in
Bangkok (cheap, everywhere), not in Paris (expensive, imported). Seasonal
eating is healthier and more sustainable.

**Approach:**
Add `location` to `user_preferences`:

- `country_code: str` (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, e.g. "TH", "FR", "GB")
- `city: Optional[str]` (free text, e.g. "Bangkok", "Paris")

Claude prompt injection block (after food profile, before recipe context):

```
USER LOCATION: Bangkok, Thailand
INGREDIENT CONTEXT:
- Prioritise locally abundant ingredients: tropical fruits, coconut,
  lemongrass, galangal, Thai basil, morning glory, papaya
- Coconut is cheap and widely available — use freely
- Avoid ingredients that are expensive or hard to source locally:
  kale, blueberries, avocado (seasonal/imported)
- Current season: [calculated server-side from country_code + month]
- Prefer ingredients that are in season in this region
```

Season is calculated server-side from `country_code` + current month —
no AI call needed, pure lookup table per hemisphere + climate zone.

**UI:** Country dropdown + optional city field in Preferences page, below
the food profile questionnaire. Auto-detect via browser `navigator.language`
as a suggested default, user confirms.

**Files to modify:**

- `backend/models/user.py` — add `country_code`, `city`
- `backend/schemas/user.py`
- `backend/services/ai/claude_generator.py` — location prompt block
- `backend/utils/seasonality.py` (new) — season lookup by country + month
- `frontend/app/(dashboard)/preferences/page.tsx`
- New Alembic migration
